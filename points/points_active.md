Modes from `merge_cell_v4sa.v`: A only, B only (the other input is never accepted), arbitrate (one at a time; the loser gets priority next time, the first tie goes to A), join (waits for both, outputs A OR B bit by bit, takes both). ## #1044 -- TEACHING MANUAL PART 6: REMEMBERING, LATCH AND SEQUENCER (Alan's direction)

## 1068. Division as a runtime loop in the VM: repeated subtraction works and is tiny, but it is slow and narrow -- the 32-step unrolled design stays the general answer (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

Alan asked for the loop version of division ("or a multiply loop as a side trick"). Built and measured in the VM only; no RTL, no hardware.

**Built:** #518's shape in the VM -- three cells: SUB (accumulator, step = divisor), BR (branch cell, held reference A-B, "continue" routed to SUB and to Q), Q (accumulator, step 1, the quotient). 3 cells instead of ~23,000. Quotient = Q, remainder = A - SUB. Test: `tests/vm/test_division_loop_v1.py` (5 tests pass).

**Harness steps, stated plainly:** (1) A-B is worked out outside and set as BR's held reference on the cell directly (as #518 did with a host-precomputed constant); a formula would need a subtract cell and a sequenced first arrival to do this inside the design -- not built. (2) SUB's freeze input is driven each tick: unfrozen for the first tick, then frozen unless BR is holding a continue.

**Measured:** 157 cases (150 random with A up to 3000, B 1..255, plus edge cases) all equal Python's quotient and remainder. Without the freeze gate the quotient is exactly one too many and the remainder is short by B -- the same race #518 found in RTL (a stale value is offered twice), now reproduced in the VM. The startup window matters: unfrozen for one tick works; two ticks fails every case with quotient 1 (found by testing, not predicted). Speed: 2 VM ticks per unit of quotient, so time grows with the answer (quotient 53 took 106 ticks).

**Limits found (all real):** the accumulator step is 8 bits (`& 0xFF`) and its threshold 16 bits, so the divisor must be 1..255 here; the number of passes equals the quotient, which for a 32-bit dividend can be 2^32 passes; divide by zero NEVER halts (checked: the counter keeps climbing), so a loop version needs an explicit guard to match the front end's "x/0 = all ones". Bit-serial long division (32 passes always) needs three circulating words plus a counter (merge + branch cells per variable); that dataflow loop is still not built.

**The multiply question:** not wrong, but it does not replace the loop. A multiply-based divide needs a reciprocal (Newton iteration, which is itself a loop) and the high half of a product, then a correction step; it is the right tool for a CONSTANT divisor (reciprocal worked out at compile time) and is NOT built or measured here. ## 1077. K (quotient bits per clock) becomes a design-time setting of the divider, at one fixed place in the record (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

## 1078. The arithmetic plan library, first plan: Horner polynomial (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

## 1079. Fixed-range \sum and \prod, chain plans, dot products (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

Next stage of the plan library after Horner (#1078). `\sum_{i=a}^{b} body` and `\prod_{i=a}^{b} body` with whole-number bounds (at most 64 terms) are parsed and written out as a chain; the library gains `sum_chain` and `product_chain` (base `acc' = acc + term` / `acc * term`, n-1 operations). The index may stand alone, be a subscript (`a_i` becomes the variable `a_k`) or an exponent (`x^i`). The body is the next multiplicative term (brackets for a sum of terms); the index is local (an `i` outside is an ordinary variable). A run-time range, an empty range, more than 64 terms, a multi-digit bound without braces, and a malformed range are refused with a reason. A polynomial produced by a sum (`\sum_{i=0}^{4} x^i`) is recognised and goes through the Horner choice of #1078 (44 cells). Dot product is `\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i`.

**Measured (compiled cells, routed_x):** chain of adds over plain variables: exactly 6 cells per term (n = 2..9: 8, 14, ..., 50). A balanced tree of adds is much worse (4 terms 29 against 20; 8 terms 101 against 44; 16 terms 288 against 92) -- I did not investigate why; the chain is the shape. Dot product, n = 2..9: 29, 53, 89, 137, 188, 260, 326, 413 cells, increments 24, 36, 48, 51, 72, 66, 87 -- NOT linear; at 8 terms 296 of 326 cells are `ram` relay cells (plus 15 priority, 8 mul, 7 adder). The order of the operations in the IR makes no difference (three orders tried, identical counts). The cost is the general placer routing 2n inputs. Even `a*b + c` is 15 cells (priority cells and relay delay lines around the mul). A pre-laid-out multiply-add stage joined N times (inputs, mul, add, one relay to carry the running total, about 5 cells per stage by my sketch -- NOT built or measured) would be linear; that is the "stored block with a join rule" route and it needs the tile-placement machinery (priority cell protocol, relay timing) driven by hand.

**Checks:** `tests/vm/test_latex_sum_prod_v1.py` (14 tests): values against Python incl. nested sums and brackets; random values with wrap-around; 8 malformed-range refusals; index locality; chain plans emit exactly the IR the front end writes; sum chain exactly 6n-4 cells and the tree larger. Mutations caught: range off by one, subscript off by one, product treated as sum. The 110 existing LaTeX tests and the 17 Horner tests pass. VM check only for the new tests (no RTL run of a dot product).

**Not done:** matrices and matrix-vector (the front end has a single output; several outputs needs a design decision); the pre-laid-out MAC block; run-time sums (loop ring); RTL runs of sums; cardinal cells.

## 1080. The stored multiply-accumulate block: a pre-laid-out stage joined N times (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

## 1081. The ramp pipe: Alan's feed pipe for matrices, large form (Alan/Claude, 2026-10-12)

**#1110 (12 Oct) -- EVERY RING NEEDS A CONTROL MECHANISM: STOP INSIDE THE GENERATED FLEX RING.** Alan's ruling: "in all of the ring designs we have to have a control mechnism, or in the case of the sub, it will runaway". *Rule.* A generated ring is not allowed without a stop. `project_assemble_v1.py -s flex --allow-rings` now REFUSES unless `--ring-steps N` is given ("the design has a ring and no control mechanism"). The stop is hardware in the design, built from existing cells ("a core over a specialist cell"): an `accumulator_cell_v4sa` counts results (pulse mode, compare value N), a `latch_cell_v4sa` holds the stop level, and the ring-side consumer of the observation fork is not offered the item while the level is set (back pressure). The host sees the Nth result at its port (held until acked) and a level `ring_stopped`; a pulse `ring_release` clears the latch and the ring continues from the same state. The ring-side offer is delayed `RING_LAG` clocks after the host takes the result so the stop lands on exactly the Nth result.

*Measured (generated RTL, iverilog).* RING_LAG 0 lets one result too many through (N+1 results); 1 is the smallest that lands exactly, for N = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20. Set to 1. Cost: 44 clocks per step instead of 42 (the lag sits on the ring). With a stop at N: exactly N results, each equal to `looped_cordic_v1.model`, `ring_stopped` rises, nothing further comes out, the Nth word is held. After `ring_release`: results N+1 onward equal the uninterrupted model. Stop at 50 and release, from the VM step-300 word 8138 and the Tang step-600 word -5748: all the way to step 1000 = 7170. Negative control: RING_LAG 0 overruns by one (test). Without `--ring-steps`: refused.

The sub family is NOT done and this is the important part of the ruling: a sub ring has fixed 1-clock latency and no ack, so nothing stops it; it needs (1) the ring period known exactly (hop count from the latency model), (2) a gated entry (merge #924 exists as a free OR) and (3) a control that PHYSICALLY breaks the loop (a branch cell v4s), so that a word cannot circulate unseen.
