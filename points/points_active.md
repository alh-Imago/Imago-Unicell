Modes from `merge_cell_v4sa.v`: A only, B only (the other input is never accepted), arbitrate (one at a time; the loser gets priority next time, the first tie goes to A), join (waits for both, outputs A OR B bit by bit, takes both). ## #1044 -- TEACHING MANUAL PART 6: REMEMBERING, LATCH AND SEQUENCER (Alan's direction)

## #1064 -- THE COMPILER'S ROUTED PLACER CAN NOW USE THE CROSSING TILE: a 10-iteration CORDIC, written as plain LLVM IR, compiles in under a second and runs bit-exact in the VM and in the generated flex RTL

Date: 2026-10-11. Alan asked for the cross and corner tiles on the flex side and for the compiler and planner to use them ("patch the compiler and the assembler, maybe the planner too"). Checked first, before building: the cross tile (core `cross`, core_select 10) and the corner tile (core 11) already exist in the ICM format, in the flex netlist step (`splice_crosses`), in FlexGrid and in the hand-layout tool `tools/flex_layout_v1.py` (#999, #1036). What was missing was the LLVM compiler's own placer: `vix_virtual_layout_v1.compile_dag_routed` refused any program whose routes must cross ("a non-planar program cannot be routed on a plane with no crossover cell"). A CORDIC needs a crossing from its second iteration, because x and y feed each other every stage. That refusal is what stopped the manual-only attempt (an outside run of the 2026-10-11 CORDIC comparison, see below).

**What changed (clone-and-increment, v1 untouched):**
- `nano/vix_virtual_layout_v2.py` (new, a clone of v1): the negotiated-congestion router now treats a cell shared by EXACTLY TWO routes, both passing straight through it, one W-E and one N-S, as a legal crossing and not a conflict (`_legal_crossings`); `_lower` turns each such cell into ONE `cross` record (`upstream_mask` / `downstream_mask` of the two passes) instead of two relay rams; `compile_dag_routed(..., allow_cross=True)` is the default in v2 and `allow_cross=False` reproduces v1's refusals. A route end (first or last cell, which touches a node pin) is never a crossing. A crossing costs one tick per direction like the relay it replaces, so no route gets longer. The failure message now says how many shared cells are legal crossings and what the other conflicts are.
- `nano/llvm_dag_frontend_v1.py`: ONE additive change, a new placer name `routed_x` that calls the v2 placer. `auto` (the default) and `routed` are unchanged, so no existing program changes behaviour. (Not cloned to a v2 frontend: the change is a four-line option, default off. Say if you want the frontend cloned instead.)
- `tests/vm/flex_rtl_harness_v1.py`: a real bug found on the way and fixed: the port-name regexes `in_(\w+)_data` / `out_(\w+)_data` also matched inside `out_main_r_data` (the letters `in_r_data` of "ma-in_r_data"), so any design with an output cell called `main.<name>` was reported as having extra input streams. Now `\bin_` / `\bout_`. The same unanchored pattern is still in `tests/vm/test_playout_v1.py`, `test_priority_flex_v1.py`, `test_sd_spi_v1.py`, `test_spi_bridge_v1.py` and `tools/sd_unit_top_v1.py` (not changed; their designs have no such names today).
- `tests/vm/test_vix_cross_router_v1.py` (new, 7 tests): v1 and v2-without-crossing both refuse the 2-iteration CORDIC, v2 places it with cross tiles; the placed design equals the integer CORDIC model in the step simulator (6 items) and in the generated flex RTL (8 items back to back, iverilog); a program that needs no crossing places cell for cell the same in v1 and v2; `auto` still refuses it; the crossing rule itself (same-way sharing, a turn and a route end are not crossings). Mutation check: lowering the crossing cells as ordinary relays makes the compile fail, caught.

**Measured (all on the multiply-by-d form of a 16-bit-state CORDIC written as LLVM IR with only add, sub, mul, shl, lshr; 32-bit words; Gowin `synth_gowin -nowidelut`):**

| iterations | result | cells | cross tiles | compile time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | v1 and v2 place it | 96 | 0 | 0.2 s |
| 2 | v1 REFUSES; v2 places it; VM bit-exact (8 items); generated flex RTL bit-exact (8 items) | 304 | 6 | 0.3 s |
| 4 | VM bit-exact (6 items) | 688 | 22 | 0.4 s |
| 10 | VM bit-exact (4 items, latency 352 ticks); generated flex RTL bit-exact, items back to back (8 items) | 1,840 | 70 | 0.8 s |

The 10-iteration design through the flex assembler for the Tang Nano 20K MAN: 1,792 cells, 37 forks, 118 joins; Yosys totals 4,319 LUT (LUT1 to LUT4) + 1,077 ALU + 35,264 flip-flops + 20 MULT36X36 + 10 MULT18X18. That is more flip-flops than the card has (15,552 in the MAN), so the full 10-stage flex line does not fit the Tang Nano 20K; no place-and-route was run (275 port bits against 266 free pins; a registered wrapper would be needed). The sub family refuses it: 40 multipliers, 12 DSP blocks (the multiply-by-d form uses 4 per iteration). Timing and throughput of the 10-stage design were NOT measured: in the step simulator items spaced 30 or 50 ticks apart come out wrong, 500 apart come out right; the safe spacing in between was not searched.

**The manual's own form (`icmp` + `select`):** with the arithmetic shift written as a biased logical shift it now places and runs bit-exact at 1, 2 and 4 iterations (1,211 cells at 2, 2,761 at 4, no multipliers, 63 cross tiles at 2; 2 iterations bit-exact in the VM, 4 only compiled). With `ashr` it places at 1 iteration and FAILS from 2: the compiler splits `ashr` into a `lo` and a `sign` piece, so one value feeds FOUR consumers and the placer's limit is three (a producer has three output faces). That limit is the next compiler fix (a fan-out relay tree for values with more than three consumers); crossing support cannot help it. The 10-iteration select form with the biased shift ALSO compiles (7,099 cells, 223 cross tiles; the compile ran for several minutes) and is bit-exact in the VM on 3 items (latency 1,012 ticks, against 352 for the multiply form); it was not run in RTL. The 10-iteration `ashr` attempt ran 4 minutes 25 seconds and failed on the 4-consumer limit.

**Not done / limits:** the `corner` tile is not used by the router yet (every remaining conflict in the cases tried was a fan-out sharing its first cell, not a turn pair; corner would matter for two routes that both turn in one square); the planner (`vix_dag_dispatcher_v1`, the growth placer) is unchanged; no teaching-manual text changed; VM and iverilog only, nothing on silicon; all area numbers are for the small-card flex/sub test vehicle, not the carrier line.

**Context (for the record, not a repo claim):** the same session compared the Verilog and UniCell CORDIC. On the repo's own 4-stage z-convergence example (re-synthesised through an independent flow): hand Verilog 175 to 183 LUT-eq at 189 to 197 MHz; UniCell sub 580 LUT-eq at 277 to 305 MHz (the repo's own run counted 1,090); flex 636 at 255 to 278 MHz and half the throughput.

## #1066 -- THE LATEX FRONT END, FIRST SLICE: integer formulas in, a design out, CHECKED against a plain Python evaluation before it is returned

Date: 2026-10-11. Alan: "Spacing and speed are secondary to correctness" and "start with the basics, that's the foundation". Built the foundation with correctness as the rule: the front end refuses what it cannot do exactly and compares every design with an independent evaluation of the same formula before handing it back. Alan's prior point stands behind it: the product is for people who are not programmers, so the messages say what is wrong and where.

**What changed (new files only; nothing existing edited except the manual text):**
- `nano/latex_frontend_v1.py`: a strict tokenizer and recursive-descent parser for integer LaTeX (whole numbers; one letter = one variable and letters side by side are a product as in maths; `\mathrm{name}`, subscripts, Greek letters; `+ - * \cdot \times`, unary minus, implicit multiplication; `( ) [ ] { } \left \right`; whole-number powers 0..64; optional `name =` label; `$...$`, `\(...\)` wrappers), a syntax tree, constant folding, an ORACLE (`evaluate`, plain Python integers reduced mod 2^32 at every step), LLVM IR generation (add, sub, mul only; same sum or product built once; powers by square-and-multiply; `5 - x` as `x*-1 + 5` so the minuend rule is never in play; the returned value is always `%r`), and `compile_latex()` which compiles through `placer="routed_x"` and then runs corner and random vectors through the compiled design in the standard VM, raising `LatexCheckError` on any disagreement (nothing is returned). Everything outside the subset raises `LatexError` with a plain reason and a caret at the position: division and `\frac`, roots, functions, sums, decimals, commas in numbers, negative or variable exponents, `x^23` without braces (LaTeX takes one digit), power of a power, absolute value, comparisons, unknown commands, numbers over 32 bits, a formula with no variable.
- `nano/latex_cli_v1.py`: command line (`--set NAME=VALUE`, `-o file.icm`, `--show-ir`, `--no-check`); exit 0 ok, 1 outside the subset or refused, 3 check failed, 2 usage.
- `tests/vm/test_latex_frontend_v1.py` (52 tests, all pass in 16 s): 13 hand-worked answers; 32-bit wrap; 27 refusals each with its reason; the caret position; 80 random formulas written as LaTeX AND as Python expressions, the Python one evaluated with exact big integers mod 2^32, at least 45 compiled and agreed on corner and random vectors; the built-in check cannot be bypassed (corrupting the runner raises); three formulas streamed through the flex step simulator at the safe spacing and through generated flex RTL (iverilog), all equal the oracle; the command line. Mutation checks: swapping the operands of a subtraction, and replacing the multiply in the power chain with an add, are both caught.
- `sub/README.md`: a new section "Writing a formula in LaTeX" (what it understands, what it refuses, how it is checked); `docs/manual.html` rebuilt.

**Decisions taken (so Alan can overrule):** two letters side by side are a product, as in LaTeX (so a longer name must be `\mathrm{total}`; the error for `total = ...` says so); 32-bit wrap-around is the number system, as in the cells; the first version refuses division and everything fractional rather than approximate it; `ret` is always a computed cell (a bare `x` becomes `x + 0`). Streaming spacing uses `max(500, 3 x cells)` ticks as a CONSERVATIVE rule of thumb (500 was right for the 10-stage line in #1064, 30 and 50 wrong); it is not a measured minimum.

**Not done / limits:** no division, fixed-point, comparisons, choices, sums, shifts or several outputs; timing and the true safe item spacing are unmeasured; the check runs in the standard VM (the flex step simulator and generated RTL are exercised in the tests, not on every compile); a formula the placer cannot lay out is reported with the compiler's own wording (plain advice added in #1065) and is not retried with other settings; no hardware.
