One cell on its own is not much. The power is in chaining: the output of one cell goes to the input of the next. In the flex family every connection is a dedicated point-to-point link between one output port and one input port, with no shared bus. Build a chain below and watch a number go through it.

1. Build a chain

Drag a cell from the palette into the grid, or click a cell and then click a slot (this works on a touch screen too). Operating cells go in the lower row, the chain. Constants (fixed ram cells) go in the upper row and feed down into the cell below them. Drag a placed block to move it.

2. What to notice

  • An empty slot in the chain just carries the value along. The real composer fills the gaps between cells with relay cells in the same way, and they take time too.
  • A two-input cell waits for both. Remove the constant above an adder and the adder never fires, so nothing reaches the output. This is the two-arrival rule from the Tour.
  • The ports are fixed. An adder has two input ports (A from the left, B from above) and one output. Nothing decides at run time where a value goes; it was fixed when the chain was built.

A simplified picture. Here each cell's output goes to its right-hand neighbour and a constant feeds down from above; the real composer can place cells anywhere and lays a route of relay cells between any two of them. Numbers are 18 bits wide and shown as signed, so they wrap around: the page computes each cell from the rules in its Verilog (adder A − B in two's complement, multiplier keeps the low half of the product, compare is a signed ≥, shift stage is a logical shift by a fixed amount, nano as in the Tour), not by running the VM.
