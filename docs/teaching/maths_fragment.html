Three cells do arithmetic: the adder, the multiplier and the accumulator. On the Chaining page they were blocks that just gave an answer. Here you can see what is inside the answer: the carry that travels along an add, the half of a product that does not fit, and a total that keeps counting.

1. The adder: bit by bit, with a carry

The adder adds two 18-bit words. Each column adds its two bits and the carry from the column to its right, writes the answer bit, and passes a carry on to the left. In subtract mode the cell adds A to the flipped bits of B and starts with a carry of 1, which is how two's complement subtraction works (A − B = A + NOT B + 1). Whatever carries out of the top column can come out on a second port, if the design was built with that port.

2. The multiplier: the half that does not fit

Multiplying two 18-bit numbers gives a product up to 36 bits long. The cell's main output is the low half, the bottom 18 bits. The high half, the top 18 bits, is available on an optional second port. The cell treats its two inputs as plain unsigned numbers (0 to 262143). If you only use the low half, you are assuming the answer fits.

3. The accumulator: a total that keeps counting

An accumulator is a running total. It has two pulse inputs, count up and count down, and each pulse changes the total by a fixed step set when it was built. In continuous mode every pulse is reported. In pulse mode nothing is reported until the total's size (ignoring sign) reaches a threshold: then the cell reports the value that crossed it and starts again from zero. As with the latch, the total always updates, but a new report can only be made once the last one has been taken.

4. What to notice

  • A carry is how a big number is built from small steps. Each column only knows two bits and a carry. A carry that ripples across all columns is why long adds take longer than short ones, and why wide adders chain carry outputs into carry inputs.
  • Subtract is add in disguise. Flip B's bits, start the carry at 1, and the same adder subtracts. When subtracting, a carry out of 1 means there was no borrow: A was at least B (read as unsigned).
  • Overflow is not an error signal. The cell gives the 18 low bits whatever happens. Two large positive numbers can give a negative result; the page flags this, but the cell itself does not.
  • Low half and high half are the same product. Throw the high half away and the answer is only right when the high half is zero. The low half is the same whether you read the numbers as signed or unsigned, which is why a signed multiply works on the Chaining page. The high half is not: it belongs to the unsigned product.
  • The accumulator is memory with arithmetic. It keeps a total, like the latch keeps a bit. In pulse mode it turns many small events into one report, which is how a counter becomes a "something has happened enough times" signal.
