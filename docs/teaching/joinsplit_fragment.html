Two small cells that look almost too simple: merge brings two paths into one, and router sends one path to up to two places. Between them they show the two ways data can meet or part, and what has to wait for what.

1. The merge cell: two paths become one

The merge cell has two inputs, A and B, and one output. It has four modes, chosen when the design is built:

  • A only and B only: a plain pass-through of one input. The other input is never accepted.
  • Arbitrate: either input may go, one at a time. If both are waiting, one wins, and then the loser gets priority next time, so neither can starve. Right when the two paths are alternatives that join again.
  • Join (OR): waits for both inputs, then outputs A OR B, bit by bit, and takes both together. Right when the two paths are two halves of one item.

Why not just OR the two whenever they arrive? Two separate items that happen to be waiting together would be fused into one wrong value. Arbitrate and Join are the safe versions of that idea.

A
B
out

    2. The router cell: one path goes to two places

    The router copies every value it takes to each of its enabled outputs, A and B. Which outputs are enabled is fixed when the design is built. It does not look at the value: it never decides, it just copies. (Deciding is the branch cell's job.)

    The rule to watch: the router takes a new value only when every enabled output has been taken by whoever it feeds. A disabled output never holds anything up. A slow or stuck receiver on one output holds back the other as well. Each receiver below can be set to accepting or stuck.

    Try:
    waiting
    router
    output A
    output B

    3. What to notice

    • Merge is where order can be lost. Two separate paths have no common clock, so the merge can only choose one at a time. That is why the page says "join" for halves of one item: it waits for both and cannot get the pairing wrong.
    • Fair is a design choice. Arbitrate hands priority to whoever just lost. Compare the strict priority cell on the Branching page, where a busy face can starve the rest.
    • Waiting is how the cells stay correct. The router never drops a value and never overwrites one. If any enabled output is stuck, everything behind it waits, and that stillness is the whole mechanism.
    • The router copies; the branch chooses. A router sends the same value to everything enabled, every time. A branch sends a value to different places depending on the value.
